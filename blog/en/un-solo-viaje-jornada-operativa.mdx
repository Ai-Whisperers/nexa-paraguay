## Introduction

One of the most common questions from prospective clients is: "How many trips to Paraguay do I need to make?" The answer, for most people, is one. With proper preparation, a single 3–4 day trip is enough to complete all in-person requirements for residency, company incorporation, and banking. This article explains exactly how that works.

## What Needs to Happen Before You Travel

The success of a one-trip operational day depends entirely on what happens before you board the plane. The following must be completed 2–6 weeks before travel:

1. **All apostilles obtained.** Birth certificate, marriage certificate, criminal record from your home country — apostilled and ready.
2. **Document review.** Your Paraguayan team reviews every document for completeness, consistency, and compliance with Migraciones requirements.
3. **Sworn translations initiated.** Documents are sent to the translator so translations are ready by the time you arrive.
4. **Appointments scheduled.** Police clearance, judicial records, notary, and Migraciones submission appointments are pre-booked.
5. **Power of attorney drafted.** If you are incorporating a company, the POA for your legal representative is prepared in advance.
6. **Bank introductions arranged.** If opening a bank account, preliminary approval is sought and the meeting is scheduled.

If any of these steps are incomplete, your trip may be partially wasted. A "one-trip" plan is only as strong as its preparation.

## The Operational Day: Hour by Hour

### Day 1: Arrival and Preparation

Arrive at Silvio Pettirossi International Airport. Our driver meets you and takes you to your hotel in Villa Morra or Carmelitas. Evening is spent confirming the next day's appointments.

### Day 2: The Core Operational Day (8:00–17:00)

| Time | Activity | Location | Duration |
|------|----------|----------|----------|
| 8:00–9:30 | Police clearance certificate | Policía Nacional, Asunción | 1.5 hours |
| 9:45–11:00 | Judicial records certificate | Corte Suprema de Justicia | 1.25 hours |
| 11:15–12:00 | Medical certificate | Designated clinic | 45 minutes |
| 12:00–13:30 | Lunch break | Nearby restaurant | 1.5 hours |
| 13:30–14:30 | Notary signing (company incorporation, POA) | Notary office | 1 hour |
| 14:45–16:30 | Residency submission at Migraciones | Migraciones office | 1.75 hours |
| 16:30–17:00 | Bank introductory meeting (if pre-arranged) | Bank branch | 30 min |

This schedule works when there is a dedicated coordinator managing logistics — someone who arrives early at each location, confirms the appointment, and signals you when to arrive.

### Day 3: Buffer and Bank Account

Not everything always goes to plan. Day 3 serves as a buffer:

- **Morning:** Complete any step that was delayed on Day 2. Common delays include the police clearance system being down (rare but possible) or needing additional copies of documents.
- **Afternoon:** Bank account opening meeting with the compliance officer. This takes 30–60 minutes if pre-approval was obtained. Without pre-approval, expect 90 minutes and a longer decision timeline.

### Day 4: Departure

Optional morning for any remaining errands. Most clients depart satisfied that everything is in motion.

## What Happens After You Leave

The work continues without your physical presence. Your Paraguayan team:

- Monitors the Migraciones application and follows up as needed
- Receives the residency resolution (typically 45–60 days)
- Processes the cedula (national ID) on your behalf via power of attorney
- Completes the RUC registration for your company
- Receives your cedula and ships it to your home address

A well-managed process means you never need to return to Paraguay for paperwork. Many clients return later as a choice — to explore more of the country, finalize a property purchase, or simply enjoy Asunción without the pressure of appointments.

## What Cannot Be Done in One Trip

Some things genuinely require more presence:

- **Property purchase:** Requires property visits, which cannot be compressed into a single operational day. Most clients make a separate trip for property.
- **Moving household goods:** Shipping furniture, vehicles, or personal effects is a separate logistics process.
- **School enrollment for children:** Requires school visits, interviews, and placement testing — typically a separate trip.

## When Two Trips Make Sense

For more complex cases, two trips spaced 3–4 months apart may be better:

- **Trip 1 (3–4 days):** Document submission, notary signing, bank introduction
- **Trip 2 (2–3 days):** Cedula collection, school visits, property viewing, settling in

Clients with families, multiple properties, or complex business structures often prefer this approach. It eliminates the pressure of a single high-stakes day.

## What Makes One Trip Work

- **Experienced local coordination.** Someone who has done this 50+ times, not someone learning on your dime.
- **Pre-booked appointments.** Walking in without an appointment is gambling. Pre-booked slots are the difference between a smooth day and a wasted one.
- **Document readiness.** Every document checked, every apostille verified, every translation completed before arrival.
- **Flexible buffer time.** Building in Day 3 as a contingency is not pessimism — it is realism.
