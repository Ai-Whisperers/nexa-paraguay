## Introduction

Paraguay's tax system is the primary reason Europeans are looking at the country in 2026. But understanding how it actually works — not the marketing version, but the real mechanics — is essential before making a decision. This article explains the territorial system, the 10% rate, VAT, and what tax residency really means.

## The Territorial System

Paraguay operates a territorial (source-based) tax system. This means only income generated within Paraguayan territory is subject to Paraguayan taxation. Income earned outside Paraguay — foreign salary, overseas investments, rental income from properties abroad, foreign business profits — is not taxed in Paraguay.

This is the fundamental difference between Paraguay and nearly every European country, which tax worldwide income. If you are a Spanish tax resident, Spain taxes your global income. If you are a Paraguayan tax resident, Paraguay taxes only your Paraguayan-source income.

The key legal concept is "source." Income is considered Paraguayan-source if the activity producing it occurs in Paraguay. A remote worker employed by a Dutch company, working from Asunción, is generating foreign-source income — not taxable in Paraguay. A consulting project for a Paraguayan client, however, is Paraguayan-source income.

## The 10% IRE Rate

IRE (Impuesto a la Renta Empresarial) is the corporate income tax rate — 10%. This is a flat rate on net profits from Paraguayan-source business activities. It applies to both companies and individuals engaged in business activities. For individuals with only employment income, the rate is also 10% (IRP — Impuesto a la Renta del Personal).

Compare this to corporate tax rates in Europe: Germany (~30%), France (~25%), Netherlands (~25.8%), Spain (~25%). The difference compounds significantly over time.

## VAT (IVA)

Paraguay's VAT is called IVA (Impuesto al Valor Agregado). The standard rate is 10%, with a reduced rate of 5% for certain essential goods and services. Export services are zero-rated, meaning you can claim input VAT credits on related expenses.

For a foreign investor moving to Paraguay, IVA is largely invisible — it is embedded in the prices you pay for goods and services. The main impact is on businesses, which must register for IVA, issue tax receipts, and file monthly returns.

## Withholding Taxes

Dividends distributed by a Paraguayan company to its shareholders are subject to a 15% withholding tax if paid to foreign residents, and 5% if paid to a local individual. Interest payments to foreign lenders are generally subject to 15% withholding, though this may be reduced under applicable tax treaties.

Paraguay has a growing network of double taxation treaties, including agreements with Spain, Chile, Uruguay, and several other LATAM countries. However, there is no treaty with the Netherlands, Germany, or the UK — meaning treaty benefits are not available for most European investors.

## Filing and Compliance

Tax returns in Paraguay are filed annually, with monthly advance payments (anticipos) for established businesses. The tax year runs from January to December. Filing is done online through the SET portal (Marangatu). The system is fully digital, but the interface is in Spanish only.

For a new resident with only foreign income, the filing obligation is minimal — you file a zero or near-zero annual return declaring no Paraguayan-source income. This is straightforward but must be done correctly to maintain compliance.

## Common Misconceptions

**"I pay 0% as a foreigner."** Not exactly. You pay 0% on foreign income, but if you earn money from Paraguayan clients or operate a Paraguayan company, you pay the applicable rates.

**"I don't need to file taxes."** You do. Even with zero local income, you must file an annual declaration. Failure to file can result in fines and complications with residency renewal.

**"Territorial means no tax reporting at all."** Paraguay does not require you to declare foreign assets or income on your local return. However, if you are a tax resident, your country of origin may still require reporting under its own rules.
