## Introduction

The single biggest bottleneck in any Paraguay relocation is document preparation. You cannot expedite this step once you are in the country. Every missing apostille, every untranslated certificate, every document that needs re-issuing from your home country costs you weeks. This checklist is designed to be printed and checked off before you book your flight.

## Documents That Must Be Apostilled in Your Home Country

These documents require a Hague Apostille from the country where they were issued. This must be done before you leave. Paraguayan authorities will not accept documents without it.

| Document | Apostille Required | Sworn Translation Needed | Notes |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------|
| Birth certificate | Yes | Yes | Must be an official certified copy |
| Marriage certificate (if applicable) | Yes | Yes | Both civil and religious if applicable |
| Criminal record / police clearance | Yes | Yes | Must be issued within 90 days of travel |
| University degrees and transcripts | Yes | Yes | Needed if applying for professional visas |
| Bank reference letters | Yes | Yes | From your home bank, dated within 30 days |
| Proof of income or net worth | Yes | Yes | Tax returns, investment statements, or pension docs |

## Documents to Obtain in Paraguay

Once you arrive, you will need to obtain several local documents. These cannot be prepared in advance.

- **Certificado de Antecedentes Policiales** (local police clearance) — obtained at the Policía Nacional in Asunción. Takes one business day.
- **Certificado de Antecedentes Judiciales** (judicial records) — obtained at the Corte Suprema de Justicia. Same-day service available.
- **Cédula de Identidad Civil** — the Paraguayan national ID card. Requires prior registration with Migraciones.
- **RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes)** — the tax ID number, processed through the SET (tax authority).
- **Proof of address** — a utility bill or rental contract in your name at a Paraguayan address.

## Translations: What You Need to Know

Every foreign-language document submitted to a Paraguayan government office must be translated by a Paraguayan sworn translator (traductor público). Translations done abroad or by non-certified translators are not accepted.

The sworn translator affixes their official seal and registers the translation with the Corte Suprema de Justicia. This adds 2–3 business days per document. Plan for it.

We recommend having all translations done in parallel while your apostilles are being processed. A well-coordinated timeline has translations arriving within the same week as your apostilled originals.

## Originals vs. Copies

Paraguayan bureaucrats want to see original apostilled documents. Photocopies — even notarized ones — are generally not accepted for first-time filings. Bring the originals in a dedicated folder. Keep photocopies as a backup for your own records.

After documents are registered with the relevant authority (Migraciones, SET, Registro Civil), you can request certified copies that subsequent agencies will accept. But the first submission must be originals.

## Digital Documents and QR Codes

As of 2026, some Paraguayan agencies accept apostilles with QR codes that can be verified online. This is true for digital apostilles issued by countries such as the Netherlands, Germany, and the UK. However, the acceptance is inconsistent across agencies. When in doubt, bring a physical apostille. Having both a digital and physical version is the safest approach.

## The Complete Timeline

Assuming you have all documents ready before travel, the document validation phase takes approximately:

- **Week 1:** Sworn translations (3–5 documents, 2–3 days)
- **Week 2:** Police clearance + judicial records in Paraguay (1–2 days)
- **Week 3–4:** Submission to Migraciones for residency (1 day, then wait)
- **Week 5–8:** Government processing of residency (45–60 days)
- **Week 9+:** Cedula issuance, RUC registration, bank account opening

## Common Mistakes

- Apostilling documents that have already expired (police clearances, bank letters have validity windows)
- Not ordering enough certified copies of key documents (birth certificate, marriage certificate)
- Assuming English-language documents will be accepted without translation (they will not)
- Waiting until arrival to start the document process — this adds 4–6 weeks minimum
