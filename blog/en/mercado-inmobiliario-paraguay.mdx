## Introduction

Paraguay's real estate market has attracted significant foreign capital over the past five years. But not all areas are appreciating equally, and not every purchase is a good investment. This article provides a data-driven overview of Asunción's residential market in 2026 — prices, yields, and risks — without the promotional tone common in relocation content.

## Asunción Neighborhoods: Price per Square Meter

Residential property prices in Asunción vary by a factor of three depending on the neighborhood. The following figures are approximate ranges for apartments in good condition (not luxury, not distressed):

| Neighborhood | USD/m² (apartment) | Trend | Profile |
|-------------|-------------------|-------|---------|
| Villa Morra | $1,200–$1,800 | Stable | Most popular with expats. Good infrastructure, restaurants, services. |
| Carmelitas | $1,000–$1,500 | Slight increase | Quieter than Villa Morra. Family-friendly, walking distance to shopping. |
| San Roque | $800–$1,200 | Stable | Up-and-coming. More local character, lower prices, good value. |
| Las Lomas | $1,500–$2,500 | Moderate increase | Premium area. Large houses on large plots. Expensive but still cheap by global standards. |
| Microcentro | $600–$1,000 | Stable | Downtown. Older buildings, more traffic. Lower entry price. |
| Santa Teresa | $700–$1,100 | Stable | Residential, good access to main avenues. |

To provide context: $1,500/m² in Las Lomas buys you a 200m² house for $300,000. In a European capital, $300,000 might buy you a studio apartment.

## Rental Yields

Gross rental yields in Asunción range from 5% to 8% for residential properties, depending on location and property type. This compares favorably to European capitals (2–4%) but is below what some LATAM markets offer.

| Property Type | Gross Yield | Occupancy | Tenant Profile |
|--------------|------------|-----------|----------------|
| 2-bed apartment, Villa Morra | 5–6% | High | Young professionals, expats |
| 3-bed house, Las Lomas | 4–5% | Moderate | Families, diplomats |
| 1-bed apartment, Microcentro | 6–8% | Moderate | Local workers, students |

Yields are gross — before property taxes, maintenance, management fees, and vacancy periods. Net yields typically run 3–5% after all costs. Property tax (Impuesto Inmobiliario) is negligible: approximately $200–$500 per year for a mid-range property.

## Foreign Buyer Trends

The profile of foreign buyers in Paraguay has shifted. Five years ago, the typical buyer was a retiree looking for a low-cost place to live. Today, the typical buyer is a working professional aged 30–50, often with a remote income, buying a primary residence while establishing tax residency.

The most active nationalities are Dutch, German, Swiss, and Argentine. Brazilians remain the largest foreign property owners in Paraguay, but their focus is typically on the border region rather than Asunción.

Most foreign buyers pay in cash or with financing from their home country. Local mortgage financing is available but expensive (12–18% interest rates) and typically requires a 50% down payment. Foreign buyers without local credit history generally cannot access local mortgages.

## Due Diligence Risks

Paraguay's property registry is reliable but not foolproof. The following risks require professional attention:

- **Title encumbrances.** Properties can have undisclosed liens, easements, or ownership disputes. A title search at the Registro de la Propiedad is essential.
- **Succession issues.** Properties inherited without formal division can have multiple heirs, some of whom may not have a clear title.
- **Zoning and planning.** Not all properties have clear zoning documentation. Building permits for renovations may not exist for existing structures.
- **Informal constructions.** Properties expanded without municipal permits are common. These cannot be legally sold as-is.

Every property purchase should include a legal due diligence process conducted by a Paraguayan lawyer with real estate experience.

## The Purchase Process

Buying property as a foreigner is straightforward in Paraguay — there are no restrictions on foreign ownership except for land within 50 km of international borders, which requires a special permit.

The process typically takes 30–60 days: (1) offer and negotiation, (2) due diligence, (3) purchase agreement signed before a notary, (4) payment (usually via escrow or bank transfer), (5) deed registration at the Registro de la Propiedad.

Closing costs are approximately 5–8% of the purchase price, including notary fees, transfer tax, registry fees, and legal fees.

## Market Outlook

Asunción's residential market is stable rather than booming. Prices have appreciated 15–25% in the best areas over the past three years, driven by foreign demand and limited supply of quality housing. New construction is concentrated in Villa Morra, Las Lomas, and the emerging Nu Guasu area.

The market is unlikely to replicate the dramatic appreciation seen in Medellín or Lisbon. Paraguay's economy grows steadily but not spectacularly. For a foreign buyer, the value is in the lifestyle and tax benefits, not in speculative appreciation.
