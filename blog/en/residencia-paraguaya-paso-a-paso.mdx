## Introduction

Paraguayan residency is straightforward compared to most countries, but it is not instant. Many online sources present a simplified version: "come to Paraguay, submit your documents, get residency in a month." The reality is more nuanced. This guide walks through the actual process, timeline, and common friction points — without marketing optimism.

## Types of Residency

Paraguay offers two main paths for foreigners:

- **Temporary Residency (Residencia Temporaria):** valid for two years, renewable. The most common path for professionals, remote workers, and retirees. Requires proof of income or sufficient funds.
- **Permanent Residency (Residencia Permanente):** available after two years of temporary residency, or directly for certain categories (investors, retirees with significant income). Direct permanent residency is increasingly scrutinized.

Temporary residency is the right starting point for most European applicants. After two years, you convert to permanent residency with minimal additional paperwork.

## The Real Timeline

The complete process — from document preparation to receiving your cedula — takes approximately 90–120 days. This breaks down as:

| Phase | Duration | What Happens |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| Document preparation | 2–6 weeks | Apostilles, translations, gathering all required documents in home country |
| Travel to Paraguay | 3–5 days | Police clearance, judicial records, submission to Migraciones |
| Government processing | 45–60 days | Migraciones reviews the application, issues the residency resolution |
| Cedula issuance | 2–3 weeks | After resolution, ID card production and pickup |
| **Total** | **~90–120 days** | |

No legitimate service can reduce the government processing time below 30 days. Anyone promising "residency in two weeks" is either misleading you or referring to a different procedure.

## Document Requirements

The core document package for temporary residency:

1. **Passport** — valid, with entry stamp showing recent arrival in Paraguay
2. **Birth certificate** — apostilled, translated by Paraguayan sworn translator
3. **Criminal record from home country** — apostilled, translated, issued within 90 days
4. **Criminal record from any country where you lived 6+ months in past 5 years** — same requirements
5. **Proof of income** — bank statements, tax returns, or pension statements, apostilled and translated
6. **Certificado de Antecedentes Policiales (Paraguay)** — obtained in person in Asunción
7. **Certificado de Antecedentes Judiciales (Paraguay)** — obtained in person in Asunción
8. **Medical certificate** — issued by a licensed Paraguayan doctor, obtained locally
9. **Proof of address in Paraguay** — rental contract or utility bill
10. **Photos** — passport-sized, specific format required by Migraciones

Migraciones may request additional documents depending on your profile. The complete list is confirmed during your initial document review.

## What Can and Cannot Be Expedited

**Can be expedited:**
- Document review and preparation (done pre-travel by your team)
- Same-day appointments at police records and judicial records offices
- Sworn translation (2–3 days instead of 5–7)
- Courier and logistics

**Cannot be expedited:**
- Apostille issuance in your home country (government service, fixed timeline)
- Migraciones processing of your application (statutory review period)
- Cedula production (physical card printing has a queue)

Any service claiming to expedite Migraciones processing is likely referring to the initial submission appointment, not the review period.

## The In-Person Day

The day you arrive in Asunción for your "operational day" includes the following steps:

1. **Morning (8:00–10:00):** Visit the Policía Nacional for the police clearance certificate. You need your passport and a local guide. Expect a queue.
2. **Late morning (10:00–12:00):** Visit the Corte Suprema de Justicia for the judicial records certificate. Faster than police if well-prepared.
3. **Lunch (12:00–13:30):** Break. Your team prepares files for submission.
4. **Afternoon (14:00–16:00):** Submission of your complete residency application at Migraciones. You will be fingerprinted and photographed. You receive a receipt with your application number.

This is the day your application is officially in process. Everything before this was preparation.

## After Submission

You return to your home country (or travel elsewhere) while Migraciones processes the application. Your local team monitors the status. When the resolution is issued, you have two options:

- **Return to Paraguay** to complete the cedula process in person
- **Send a power of attorney** to your team to handle the remaining steps

Most clients choose the second option, meaning only one trip is required. The cedula is then shipped internationally.

## Post-Residency Steps

Once you have temporary residency, you can:

- Obtain your cedula (Paraguayan ID)
- Register for RUC (tax ID)
- Open bank accounts
- Purchase property
- Incorporate a company

Permanent residency after two years requires: proof of continuous residence (bank transactions, utility bills, rental contract), updated criminal records, and a simpler application with fewer documents.

## Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

- **Expired documents.** Police clearance from your home country must be less than 90 days old at submission. Time your apostille carefully.
- **Missing entry stamp.** If you entered Paraguay without a stamp (bus from Argentina, for example), you need to regularize your entry before applying. This adds a week.
- **Inconsistent names.** If your name appears differently on different documents (e.g., middle initial variations), bring a sworn statement explaining the discrepancy.
- **Incomplete translation packages.** Every document in a foreign language must be translated. Missing one document can delay your application by weeks while a new translation is prepared.
