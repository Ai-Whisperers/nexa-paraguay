## Introduction

Paraguay offers two main corporate structures for foreign investors: the EAS (Empresa por Acciones Simplificada) and the SA (Sociedad Anónima). Choosing between them is one of the first and most consequential decisions you will make. This article breaks down the differences in plain language, with real requirements and timelines.

## EAS — Simplified Stock Company

The EAS was introduced in 2019 as a modern, lightweight corporate structure designed for small to medium enterprises and foreign investors. It requires a minimum of one shareholder (individual or legal entity) and one director. There is no minimum capital requirement in theory, but in practice, a capital of USD 5,000–10,000 is recommended for credibility with banks and suppliers.

**Key advantages:**
- Incorporation in 7–10 business days
- No board of directors required
- Shareholder meetings can be held virtually
- No statutory auditor required unless revenue exceeds thresholds
- Can be fully owned by a foreign entity or individual

The EAS is ideal for freelancers, remote workers establishing tax residency, small import-export operations, and holding companies for personal assets.

**Disadvantages:**
- Cannot publicly trade shares
- Less formal structure may be viewed as less credible by some institutions
- Limited to a maximum of 50 shareholders

## SA — Corporation (Sociedad Anónima)

The SA is the traditional corporate structure, more suitable for larger operations. It requires a minimum of two shareholders and a board of directors. The minimum capital is approximately USD 1,000, but for operational credibility, USD 20,000–50,000 is typical.

**Key characteristics:**
- Incorporation in 15–25 business days
- Requires a board of directors with at least two members
- Must appoint a statutory auditor (síndico)
- Shares can be transferred more easily
- More formal governance requirements
- Better suited for joint ventures, large real estate projects, and regulated industries

The SA is the preferred structure for companies seeking bank financing, participating in public tenders, or operating in regulated sectors such as insurance, finance, or telecommunications.

## The Incorporation Process

Regardless of structure, the process follows the same sequence:

1. **Name reservation.** You propose three corporate names to the Registro Público de Comercio. They check availability and reserve your chosen name for 30 days.
2. **Drafting the bylaws.** A Paraguayan lawyer drafts the estatuto social — the company's charter. This must include capital structure, shareholder details, management, and purpose.
3. **Notarization.** The bylaws are signed before a Paraguayan notary public. This requires the physical presence of all shareholders or their legal representatives with power of attorney.
4. **Registration with the Registro Público.** The notarized bylaws are submitted to the commercial registry. This step takes 5–15 days depending on workload.
5. **RUC registration.** Once the company is registered, you obtain the RUC (tax ID) from the SET. This takes 1–2 business days.
6. **Bank account.** With the RUC and registered bylaws, you open a corporate bank account.

## Timeline Reality Check

The 7–10 day EAS timeline assumes all documents are ready: apostilled and translated powers of attorney for foreign shareholders, clear corporate purpose, and no complications with name conflicts or registry backlogs. When documents are incomplete, the timeline can double.

Our experience: a well-prepared EAS incorporation with Nexa Paraguay averages 8 business days from document submission to RUC issuance. An SA averages 18 business days.

## Tax Implications by Structure

Both structures are subject to the same tax regime in Paraguay: 10% IRE (corporate income tax) on locally sourced income, 10% VAT (IVA) on taxable transactions, and withholding taxes on dividend distributions. The structure does not change your tax rate — it changes your compliance obligations. An SA requires more formal accounting, audited financial statements, and board meeting minutes.
