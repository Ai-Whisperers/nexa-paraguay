## The Dream: Residency Without Living There

Most countries require you to spend 183+ days per year within their borders to maintain residency. This is the "physical presence trap" — you gain residency on paper, but you're locked into living there full-time.

For digital nomads, investors, retirees, and anyone with a global lifestyle, this defeats the purpose. You want the benefits of residency (tax optimization, legal status, a second home) without being chained to one location.

A small group of countries offers something different: **residency with no minimum stay requirement.**

Here's the complete list in 2026.

## 1. Paraguay — The Overwhelming Winner

**Physical presence:** One visit every 3 years to renew your physical cédula. That's it. You can spend one afternoon at the migration office and leave the same day.

**Why it wins:**

- **Lowest physical presence requirement** — Every 3 years, not 2. And you don't need to stay overnight.
- **Cheapest setup** — $2,900+ all-in (lawyer fees, document apostilles, migration fees, cédula costs).
- **Best tax treatment** — 0% on all foreign income. No wealth tax. No capital gains tax on foreign assets.
- **Only country with a citizenship path** — After 3 years of permanent residency, you can apply for citizenship. None of the other "no stay" options offer this.
- **No income requirement** — Qualify on savings alone. No minimum monthly income.
- **No CRS reporting** — Paraguay doesn't share financial data with other countries.
- **Crypto-friendly** — Crypto treated as goods, 0% capital gains tax, no DAC8 reporting.

**Who it's for:** Digital nomads, remote workers, retirees, investors, crypto holders, anyone wanting a second passport.

### How It Works

1. Enter Paraguay as a tourist.
2. Your lawyer files your residency application.
3. You visit the migration office (one appointment, ~30 minutes).
4. 60-90 days later, you get your cédula (permanent residency ID).
5. Return every 3 years for a 15-minute renewal.

**That's it.** No annual renewals. No income reporting. No tax filings on foreign income.

## 2. Panama — The Runner-Up

**Physical presence:** 1 day every 2 years.

Panama's Friendly Nations Visa offers permanent residency with a minimal stay requirement. You must visit Panama once every two years to maintain your residency.

**Pros:** 0% foreign income tax, US dollar economy, strong banking, good infrastructure.

**Cons:** Higher cost ($5,000-8,000+ setup), citizenship takes 5-8 years, requires a job offer or investment for most visa categories, higher cost of living ($1,200-1,800/mo).

Panama is a strong alternative, especially for those who want proximity to North America and a more developed infrastructure. But it's more expensive upfront and takes longer for citizenship.

## 3. UAE — Zero Days, Zero Future

**Physical presence:** Can be maintained entirely remotely. No minimum stay.

The UAE offers residence visas (usually 2-3 years, renewable) tied to employment, freelancing (freelance visa), property ownership, or investment. No physical presence required.

**Pros:** True 0% tax on all income, world-class infrastructure, Dubai lifestyle, no CRS reporting.

**Cons:** Citizenship is virtually impossible regardless of how long you stay. The "residency" is a renewable visa, not permanent status. High cost of living ($1,800-3,000+). Visas require ongoing financial commitments (e.g., property, employment, or bank deposits).

The UAE works well for high earners who want zero tax and don't care about a second passport. For everyone else, it's expensive and has no exit strategy.

## 4. Thailand Elite — Expensive and Temporary

**Physical presence:** 0 days. Visa can be maintained remotely.

The Thailand Elite Visa is a program that grants long-term stays (5, 10, or 20 years) in exchange for a lump-sum fee. No physical presence requirement.

**Pros:** No physical presence, no income tax on foreign income if not remitted, good lifestyle, easy application.

**Cons:** No path to citizenship. The fee ranges from $15,000 (5 years) to $60,000+ (20 years). You're still a temporary resident. Thailand tax laws can be tricky — remitted foreign income may be taxable. Visa is tied to the program, not genuine residency.

## 5. Nicaragua — The Dark Horse

**Physical presence:** 1 visit every 2 years.

Nicaragua offers permanent residency through investment (typically $30,000+ in real estate or a business). Physical presence is minimal — one visit every two years.

**Pros:** 0% tax on foreign income (territorial system), low cost of living ($500-800/mo), citizenship in 4 years.

**Cons:** Political instability, limited healthcare infrastructure, lower internet quality, fewer expat services. Setup costs around $10,000+ depending on investment.

Nicaragua is worth considering for those on a tight budget, but the political and infrastructure risks are real.

## The Fine Print

### What "No Minimum Stay" Really Means

Every country on this list requires some form of ongoing connection. For Paraguay, it's a 15-minute cédula renewal every 3 years. For Panama, it's a quick visit every 2 years. For Thailand Elite, it's an annual renewal fee.

None of these require you to "live" there. You can spend 364 days a year anywhere else in the world.

### What Could Go Wrong

- **Policy changes** — Any country can change its rules. Paraguay's immigration framework is established in law (not a temporary program), making it more stable.
- **Tax residency audit** — If you spend 183+ days in another country, that country may claim you as a tax resident. Paraguay's 0-day requirement protects you from this.
- **Renewal complications** — Some countries make renewals bureaucratic. Paraguay's cédula renewal is straightforward — pay a fee, update fingerprints, done.
